The Ultimate Sleepaway Camp Packing Checklist

Isabel Moreau Jul 25, 2026 10 min read

Packing for a first sleepaway week is where most parents quietly spiral. After fifteen seasons of watching trunks arrive at our gates, we can predict exactly what gets forgotten — and what never needed to come at all. This checklist is the one our counselors hand out, refined by thousands of real camp weeks.

Start With The One-Bag Philosophy

If your camper can't carry it from car to cabin solo, it's too much. One wheeled trunk or duffel plus a daypack covers everything. Kids who pack light actually keep their cabins tidier — counselors swear by this — because every item has a home within reach.

"Pack outfits in labeled zip bags — one per day plus three extras. It turns morning chaos into grab-and-go independence." — Cabin Counselor Handbook, page 4

The Non-Negotiables

  • Seven tagged t-shirts, four shorts, two warm layers — mountain evenings dip below 60°F even in July.
  • Broken-in sneakers plus waterproof sandals — new shoes equal blisters by Tuesday.
  • Twin XL bedding or a sleeping bag rated to 50°F, plus a pillow from home for the homesick hours.
  • Quick-dry towel and swim kit in mesh bags — wet items need to breathe on the cabin line.
  • Headlamp, refillable bottle, sunscreen stick — the holy trinity of daily adventures.

Smart Packing Tricks Our Staff Swear By

Label everything twice: once on the tag, once inside the collar. Pack a "missing sock" pouch so singles have a meeting point. Freeze two water bottles the night before departure — they double as ice packs and cold drinks by arrival.

What To Leave Behind

We return valuables to parents every single session. Phones stay in the welcome-kit lockers anyway (our policy), expensive anything stays home, and snacks attract more wildlife than friends. One small comfort item — photo, plushie, journal — is encouraged and honored.

The Night-Before Ritual

Do a "cabin walk-through": your camper carries their own bag up and down the stairs once, finds each labeled item on demand, and signs the checklist themselves. Confidence is the heaviest thing they'll bring — and it weighs nothing.
